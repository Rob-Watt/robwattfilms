Kevin Holland preparing for UFC 334 and Taylor Sheridan’s Bosque Ranch Competition

nov 12, 2026 · rob watt

UFC fighter Kevin Holland trains cutting horses on a Texas ranch for the cutting competition at Taylor Sheridan’s Bosque Ranch, then works the mitts with Coach D at Squabble MMA, ahead of UFC 334 at Madison Square Garden on November 14, 2026.

Wolfgang and I arrived with the Hawx Workwear team at the ranch and parked our SUV in the dirt next to a horse trailer. The property was beautiful and expansive. Kevin Holland was waiting for us in his ranch cart and opened the gate. His demeanor was very calm and professional, even though you could sense a playfulness under the surface. We were there to document Kevin in his element as he prepared for UFC 334 as well as the Horse Cutting Competition at Bosque Ranch Headquarters.

In Fort Worth or Amarillo, you can’t go anywhere without someone talking about Taylor Sheridan, the Four Sixes, and Bosque Ranch. The Equestrian community is receiving a huge amount of attention at the moment thanks to his media empire. The ultimate goal is to get Horse Cutting into the Olympic Games, and considering his momentum, I wouldn’t bet against Sheridan. I once spoke with him at Sundance about Hell or High Water and his own personal journey, and knowing his story really inspired the hell out of me.

The cows were curiously observing us as we drove past, and as we pulled up to the training arena, it was clear that Kevin had surrounded himself with lifelong experts to help his preparations. Kevin carefully tacked up his horse and warmed up with a few laps around the ring. You might think that a professional martial artist shouldn’t entertain any other hobbies, but it quickly became apparent that the reaction speed that was demanded of Kevin during this horse training was going to lend him a massive advantage on fight night.

The heat was intense, the type that forms a competitive mental fortitude. Back and forth the bulls attempted to escape but Kevin’s horse was always a few steps ahead and cut off the bull’s route. Anticipation is critical. Hesitate for a split second and that bull is long gone. Nuanced leg pressure guided the horse exactly where Kevin intended. Eventually, the bull’s will toppled and he gave up, pinned in the corner.

These animals are so large that one swift shift of their body can crush a human. That instills a level of respect and awareness in the rider, if they’re smart. It also makes the thought of fighting a human seem slightly less daunting. This is the factory for grit, and Kevin doesn’t shy away from hard work.

After his horse was washed down, he drove across town to put in some rounds on the mitts. Coach D welcomed us onto the mats at Squabble MMA (SQ8BL3) to get up close and feel the intensity of Kevin hitting the pads. It is a good thing Coach D can take some punishment, because even in a padded vest, the body shots looked and sounded like they took a few years off the life expectancy. Round after round, Kevin kept going, digging deep and changing levels. Elbows, punches, knees, and kicks in rapid succession. Flow state. It was impressive. He is a fan favorite for a reason.

Kevin Holland fights this weekend at Madison Square Garden at UFC 334. His work ethic will pay off.
